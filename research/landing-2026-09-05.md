# Landing: how the real thing and the sims do it, and what noelite took from it

*2026-09-05. The research agents for this died on the session's usage limit before writing a
word, so this is from memory of the rules and the sims, not from fresh sources. Where a number
matters it is a certification figure or a widely published one; check before quoting it
anywhere that counts.*

## Touchdown

- Airline touchdowns: 60 to 180 ft/min (0.3 to 0.9 m/s). A firm one 240 (1.2). Over 600 ft/min
  (3 m/s) is logged as a hard landing and grounds the aircraft for an inspection.
- FAR 25.473 certifies transport gear to a 10 ft/s (3.05 m/s) descent at max landing weight and
  6 ft/s at max takeoff weight, with a reserve energy case at 12 ft/s. Land-based fighters run
  about 12 to 13 ft/s; carrier aircraft are designed for 22 to 25 ft/s (about 7 m/s) because a
  carrier landing is a controlled crash with no flare.
- Vref is 1.3 times the stall speed in the landing configuration (FAR 25.125); fighters fly an
  optimum angle of attack that comes out near 1.2 to 1.25 Vs.
- Flare: begin at 20 to 30 ft (light aircraft) or 30 to 50 (jets), pitch up 2 to 5 degrees,
  aim to arrest the sink to 100 to 200 ft/min at touchdown. Nose-up at touchdown of 5 to 10
  degrees is normal; tail strikes start around 10 to 12 on airliners, higher on fighters with a
  short tail.
- A bounce comes from excess sink or excess speed (the wings still flying) or an early flare;
  nose-wheel-first contact wheelbarrows and porpoises. The cure is attitude and idle power, or
  a go-around.
- Crosswind: crab up to 15 to 20 degrees on the approach, de-crab in the flare; bank at
  touchdown over 5 to 8 degrees puts a wingtip or an engine pod on the ground.
- Ground effect within one wingspan of the ground: induced drag falls and lift rises, so the
  aircraft floats; a fast approach floats a long way.

## The sims

- MSFS and X-Plane model the gear as spring-dampers with tyre contact; a hard touchdown bounces
  and over their damage thresholds (roughly 600 to 800 ft/min with damage on) the aircraft is
  broken. X-Plane also breaks aircraft on overspeed and g.
- War Thunder's arcade instructor limits angle of attack, auto-levels the wings and damps the
  controls at low speed; landing is forgiving of sink and unforgiving of speed and drift.
- Ace Combat lands you with an ILS box and an assisted final; it forgives almost everything
  inside the box and crashes you outside it.
- GTA V planes bounce readily and break on sink and roll; Pilotwings scores the landing on sink
  and centreline.

## Keyboard

- Keyboard sims ramp a virtual stick over 0.3 to 0.5 s and centre it faster, and scale control
  authority with dynamic pressure, so the same key moves the aircraft less at approach speed.
- Throttle on a keyboard is a setting (F1 to F4 presets, or plus and minus in steps), never a
  held key; an approach autothrottle holding Vref is standard on airliners and a kindness in a
  game.
